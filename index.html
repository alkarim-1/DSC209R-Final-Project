Overview

Women outlive men in nearly every country with available data, but how much longer they live and how that difference has changed over time is less obvious. This article uses World Bank data from 1960 to 2019 to ask a simple question: who outlives whom? We begin by looking at the overall rise in female and male life expectancy. We then examine how the gap between them has widened, narrowed, or changed direction in the world's most populous countries. Finally, we look at survival to age 65 to ask whether the same longevity advantage appears when survival is measured another way. Each figure is interactive, so use the sliders, Play buttons, story controls, and hover details to explore the patterns yourself.

AND

Female and male life expectancy have both increased

The first part of the story is something females and males share: people are living longer than they did several decades ago. Across countries, life expectancy increased for both groups between 1960 and 2019. This overall rise gives us the starting point for understanding the longevity gap.

BUT

Women now outlive men in the world's biggest countries

Female and male life expectancy both increased, but those gains were not equal. In 2019, women outlived men in all 30 of the world's most populous countries. That was not always true: in 1960 men lived longer in Bangladesh, India and Iran, while the gap was already around 7 to 8 years in Russia, South Korea and Vietnam. Since then, the gap has widened by more than half a year in 18 of these countries, narrowed in 6 and barely moved in 6. THEREFORE

Does the same difference appear in survival to age 65?

Life expectancy tells us how many years people are expected to live. Therefore, we look at longevity another way: who actually makes it to age 65? This visualization compares the percentage of female and male cohorts expected to survive to age 65. Countries above the equality line have higher female survival, while countries below the line have higher male survival.

Development Process

What have we done so far?

So far, we have explored World Bank data related to life expectancy and created several interactive visualizations. We selected three visuals that create an AND–BUT–THEREFORE story. Our visuals explore changes in life expectancy, the female-male longevity gap, and survival to age 65. We also added interactions such as sliders, Play buttons, hover details, country selection, and zooming.

What will be the most challenging part to design and why?

The most challenging part will be making the three visualizations feel like one connected story. Each visualization shows longevity in a different way, so the transitions between them need to be clear. We also need to make sure the interactions are useful without making the page overwhelming. Our goal is for the interactions to help explain the story instead of distracting from it.

Conclusion

Female and male life expectancy have both increased over time, but those gains have not always been equal. The longevity gap has widened in some countries, narrowed in others, and even changed direction. Looking at survival to age 65 gives us another way to see the same broader pattern: living longer is only part of the story.
